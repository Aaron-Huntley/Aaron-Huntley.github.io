# Poetry

If written language is a way to transfer information from one human to another, then what is poetry.
I want to take a perspective that poetry can be even more beautiful with this in mind.

<!-- more -->

Following ideas around thinking, thoughts at least to me seem finite.
That is they have a finite number of ideas.
Maybe thought and ideas are synonymous and im just pushing words around, but stay with me.

I have to imagine that when poetry is written theres often ideas in mind the poet is trying to capture.
Even if there isn't theres something in the poets mind while writing.
Weather thought emotion or emotion or something else the poet is still human.
That stuff in the poets mind constitutes some amount of information.
The information which is then encoded in a beautiful way as written language.
Once complete the poet is fixed with that information.

So, how can it be the information changes depending on the reader?

Often when reading a poem, it is similarly inevitable your human brain has thought, emotions ect.
Those mind instances may be the same as the poets.
For example if I wrote "A red car." and then you read it, it may just have an equivalent mind instance in my mind when I wrote to that as when you read it.

However, the thoughts may be different.
They may expand on the idea, they may be a completely different idea entirely.
However, you still attribute the ideas to the poem.
In this sense, the poem has more information that was intended.
In fact, it may even break the rules of how much information is allowed in that much data.
For each new reader with their own ideas this poem has increasingly more information.

I think this is very interesting, how can it be that the poem has so much information.
The information of the poem isn't stored in the words.
The information of the poem is stored in the minds of the poet and the reader.
I find it beautiful, that when you read a poem you are inherently the joint key keeper of the poem.
The poem is not the words but the ideas in yours and the poets mind.

Is this fact inherently human.
If you asked me 10 years ago I would have said yes, of course.
Now, i'm not sure.
It still feels very human to me, as it has just that.
Feeling.
I hope it is human.
Then we have one up on the computers.
A way to transfer information using the minimal amount of data.

I like to think we can rank poems based on their information density.
So a poem, as in the sheet of paper with words on it, is "better" if theres more ideas to words ratio than another.
Maybe, I can say "A red car." and call it a poem.
All of a sudden you might be thinking, "Wow. Such poetry, many ideas.".
If you do think that then I'm ashamed in you, I'm not a poet.
