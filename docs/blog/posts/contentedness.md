# Contentedness

Are you content with your life? 
Are you satisfied with your life?
What gives you satisfaction?
Does the contentedness bring a meaning to your life?
Does it remove the meaning?
Do you want a meaning to life?
Do you have one?

<!-- more -->

When I was younger I had specific goals.
I wanted to get a PhD and produce novel research.
I wanted to travel, meet new people and have many new and exciting experiences.
I chose to study mathematics in part to achieve this goal, but more in part because I saw something which I thought gave my life meaning.
Maths gave me tools to answer questions, to think, so I could think about what was the meaning maths gave.
I remember distinctly sitting in a class, sheet of paper in front of me, staring into the cosmos of mathematics and witnessing its endlessness.
It was a single moment which was so powerful it completely predicted the course of my life.
I'm not attributing my life to this moment, every other experience in my life has built me to have these thoughts and feelings.
However, I have thought about that moment a lot.
It feels important to me.

Years following then I had not thought much about the question of what brought meaning to my life.
Recently, I like thinking.
I have made decisions of how I want to live, what I think is correct, how certain decisions align with a moral compass I have constructed. 
I have slowly built up a picture of what it means to be me.

This is an egotistical question, that is a question of what my ego looks like.
This doesn't mean I have to get attached to it but instead observe it, question it.
It is much more comfortable, in my opinion to take an ego as the axiom and retrofit meaning.
I don't want to do that (ironically that want is also part of the ego).
I believe that asking the meaning of life globally is not a sensible question. 
However, asking it locally (what is it to you) is not only answerable but you can choose the answer.

Part of that picture is the idea of contentedness.
I want to live a life which is content and satisfied.
What do I mean by those words?

I feel like my consciousness can be split, vaguely, into two parts.
One that thinks and one that does.
I don't always do the thinks I think, or the things I want to do.
I don't always think about the things I'm doing.
So its the thinking side, lets call it the mind, which comes up with the moral compass.
The doing side, lets call it the soul, which makes the decisions.

Sometimes the mind thinks unconsciously, which almost seems like a contradiction but I think it shows how interconnected the two concepts are.
Emotions feel like they are derived from the mind.

I think it is the interplay between the mind and soul where one can find contentedness.
If the mind and soul are aligned then a person becomes content.

How does contentedness manifest in my life?

My soul manifests it by making goals, going to new places making new friends.
My mind manifests it with being present in the moment, accepting whats in front of me, observing it and my reaction to it.

Both manifestations allow me to both be satisfied with my life by my original definition of having goals and achieving them.
However, to be consistent with my minds contentedness I must accept my position in the present; to be okay in witnessing that pursuit of goals and, be okay in the failure of those goals.

If I just pursued the goals without thought of the mind then I predict the following.
I achieve them, and feel discontented after with no goals remaining.
I fail to achieve them and hence feel discontented.
I am in constant pursuit of them, where I feel contented.
The addition of the mind does not restrict contentedness but the opposite, it allows one to be content with the failure or achievement of goals.

Now it is not the goals that bring my life meaning.
It is the witnessing of myself which brings meaning.
Witnessing the pursuit of goals, enjoying the excitement of that pursuit.
Witnessing my frustration to the failure of them.
Reveling in the joy of achieving them.

I think a practical benefit of this perspective shift is that it doesn't put pressure on something outside of your control.
You don't always have autonomy of what you want to do, you have limitations as a human.
Often as humans we choose goals beyond those limitations.
My perspective is not that we should change the goals but have a contentedness with them.
We can still choose any goal, I wouldn't want to take that away from myself.
But now there is no bother depending on the outcome.
